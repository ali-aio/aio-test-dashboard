Fleet Wall — rack concepts

Two directions, both real interactive elements (hover, click-to-expand), not flat mockups. Mock battery/status data, real T7 icon asset.

A — Charging Shelf

Literal row of docked units, like the real charging station — your T7 icon upright, evenly spaced, with a soft floor shadow. Hover tilts the unit forward and lifts it slightly (like picking it up off the shelf) and reveals a tooltip card; a status LED sits at the top corner of each.

Good: instantly reads as "a shelf of T7s" — the most literal, charming interpretation of "rack of devices," and the hover-lift is a genuinely satisfying micro-interaction rather than decoration.
Bad: only works well for one device class at a time (icons for kiosk/pos don't exist yet, so a mixed fleet needs a fallback glyph) and doesn't scale past ~30-40 visible units before the shelf needs to scroll/paginate.

B — Server Rack

Datacenter rack-unit rows: icon, serial, a horizontal battery bar (color follows charge level), percentage, and a pulsing status LED — exactly the "rack of devices" reading from ops tooling. Click a row to expand a battery history chart inline, rack-style (no modal, no navigation).

Good: scales to hundreds of devices (just more rows, same row height), the horizontal bar makes scanning "which devices are low" trivial at a glance, and it's the most literal "rack" reading — dense, dark, technical.
Bad: least charming of the two — purely functional, no personality. Loses the "oh that's our actual hardware" delight that A has.
