Test Dashboard — top chrome options

Same Finder shell (chosen), three ways to do the top of the window. None of them show an app name — the window is the app. Views (Overview, Devices, Test cycles, Health, Charging, Thermal) move around; the source list, table and inspector stay as they are. Toggle dark to compare.

A · No toolbarB · Finder titlebarC · Split toolbar

A · No toolbar simplest

Nothing above the content. The source list starts with the search field and a Views section with colored glyphs (System Settings style); fleet filters and smart lists sit below it. The pane carries its own one-line header (title · count) and any page controls. Inspector toggle lives in the status bar.

ProsNo chrome above the content at all; the view list and the filter list are one column, so nothing is in two places. Most vertical room for the table. Matches the Enroll app's sidebar exactly.
ConsLongest sidebar (Views + Fleet + Smart lists + Test cycles + System) — on short screens it scrolls. Search is narrower. Views and filters look alike unless the glyphs stay colored.

B · Finder titlebar

A slim 38 px titlebar like Finder: the centered title is the current view and its count ("Devices — 26"), nothing else is text. Left: sidebar toggle. Right: search, inspector toggle. Views are a section in the source list, as in A, but the sidebar needs no search field.

ProsReads exactly like a Finder window: the title tells you where you are, the sidebar tells you where you can go. Table starts right under the header. Search keeps its full width.
ConsOne more bar than A, even if slim. The centered title is decorative on wide windows and can collide with the search field on narrow ones.

C · Split toolbar

The toolbar is split in three columns aligned with the body, like Mail or Notes: traffic lights over the sidebar, a segmented control with icons as the only thing over the pane, search + inspector toggle over the inspector. Views stay in the toolbar but with no title text or app name; the sidebar is filters only.
